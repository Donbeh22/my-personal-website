My Little Eye

Chapter 1: The Zubov Cleaner It all started when Eleanor’s only eye began to erase itself. Funny thing, that. It’s not as if it wasn’t in use. Full-time. Overheating itself with her half-baked analysis of every Buber Blob that came into view. “Useless”, she snickered, “utterly bubish blustering blubbers,” she poo-pooed. Zorg and Tumble were the only ones wading alongside her that day, on their way to Adaptive Bodies for Adaptive Habitats 8.0 — of all places. “What’s wrong, Elle?” Tumble’s left head sprang forward, getting so far ahead of his right head that it bounced wildly back. “Hm, how do you mean?” she hopped and bopped, oblivious that her eye-wiper was wiping her entire eye away. And, in one last wipe, in a single fleeting swipe, her singularly splendid eye went… “bye-bye”. What they all failed to realize was that Eleanor’s eye always had a habit of falling in, phasing out, burning up, and otherwise going on the fritz more than the others. But this time, it erased … itself. Something that hadn’t happened to their species in who-knows-how-many moon-moon winks. If ever. Who knew, they said. What could have possibly caused or reverse-caused it, they wondered. “Elle, you’re the Chosen One!” They giggled as Eleanor yanked at the air around them in search of something that was supposed to be attached, and from deep within to boot: “It can’t! It can’t! Can it?” “Well, it has. What now?” Zorg whistled, flipping his toy meteors into the air so fast their tails swooshed with extra fireballs. “Let’s take her to Sabertooth Plaza…and show everyone,” Tumble’s right head quipped. “Not…funny.” Eleanor yipped back. I’ve seen it all, or so I thought—she thought to herself. But this was a different predicament. A pickle to end all pickles. An Eyeless Farce! “You mean my eye could just fall out…and I could sell it to the highest bidder?” She flashed back to herself asking their tutor, Dressleta, one dreary morning. It was a strange habit she had, posing melodramatic, improbable scenarios to make her “education” zippier and more tolerable. “Well, now, why would it fall out, Eleanor?” Dressleta retorted, bemused, her face frozen and serene, as her eight limbs gesticulated wildly. “I dunno, isn’t that what YOU said?” Dressleta was trying to transmit — a little too delicately perhaps — that, in some younger galaxies, certain species resort to selling parts of their shells, vehicles, suits, and/or “bodies” — to survive in their shells, vehicles, suits, and/or “bodies”. It was Dressleta’s reluctance to go into specifics, her insistence on this “gentle explanation” for the fledglings of their species, that had now sent little Elle into a manic state of imagining that she was the sole cause of her loss: “Did I start all this? Did I think my eye out of existence? Was it traded in for some Mercurial SIV on a satellite planet by some exo-barbarian I’m not yet even aware of?” Zorg wept with glee as he read her panicked mind. “Except you can’t even sell it, Elle! There’s nothing left to sell. It’s invisibled-itself—into a million invisible fizzibles!” “Tst!” Elle snapped. “Yes, your EYENESS!!!!” Zorg clapped back. Tumble tsk-tsked and shook their twin heads at Zorg because, yes, even though that’s just the kind of little pukes they were. This was entirely surprising to Zorg, the tenderest-hearted of the bunch. Tumble’s mischievous galactic suit had something to add that sounded a little too much like their twin heads: “Rag off, Zorg! Go show off to your shared twizzle-poker of a girlfriend!” Zorg sighed: “Can’t control yer own suit yet, eh?! Dummy suits that roast us, what a terrible idea. Whose idea was this?” Tumble’s heads both fumed at the last quip. “Focus, Zorg! None of this makes any sense! There must be something I can do to bring it back.” Eleanor whimpered. “Frug!” Zorg suggested. “Not a frug when it’s true.” Elle clapped back. “Who says? When an entire limb exercises itself out of your experience, Dum Dum—that limb is NOT coming back! It’s a frug.” Eleanor panted and blindly reached out with her hands — F-R-UUUUUU-G? “If only we had two eyes, so that if one falls out, we could still at least rely on the other!” “Don’t be silly. No one in this blessed universe has two! It’s unheard-of!” Tumble’s right head huffed. “Unthinkable!!” Tumble’s left head shook. “Ludicrous! Impossible!” Shook the right. Elle’s Neck Sensor Bulbs would rotate when they sensed imminent danger. They were going into overdrive now that one of her many senses was gone, eliminated, done away with, POOF! Verklempt! Spuntok! All in a flash. She wondered if the ever-present Zubov Cleaner swept it away. “Zubov!!! Give it back!” She throttled and rode Zubov clear into the night, kicking and screaming, as it hiccuped and spat up a few loose, unconscious screws and then, as was its custom, continued waddling away towards its next victim—while its pulse made insulting noises on the way out, too. It wasn’t Zubov, after all. Well, if it wasn’t Zubov…who took it? Where did it go? How can I get it back? “How can I spy with my humongous eye if my humongous eye is no more?” What to do. Who to ping. Where to rip a blood-curdling WHYYYYYYYYYY!!!!!!!!!??????? You would think that a cosmically highly regarded being with a total of 52 senses could somehow calculate the coordinates of ANY missing thing with some combination of each sense. But, alas, not our poor ‘lil Elle - hers seem to all suddenly go into forced hibernation with the disappearance of that “galdurned” eye. Later that night, Eleanor seemed to hallucinate during this “unforeseen hibernation” with extra-dimensions and extra-senses, hitherto uncharted: Great dazzling balls of a Hue Unknown to the Essothorp. And a thundering voice that came from somewhere that felt both instantly nearby and impossibly far away: “YOUUUU ARE NOT YOUR EYE.” I am my eye. Without my eye, what am I? “You are NOT your eye.” The mystery voice echoed, booming with a commanding tone she suspected she had never heard before. Or had she? “ELEANOR ICKBY SUPERNETHERSHAMBLES.” “Yes?” Elle whimpered. YOU ARE NOT YOUR LITTLE “I”, Eleanor shook, reaching with all her might, hoping it was all a dream that was being downloaded from one of the constellations that the Essothorp were helping to guide to “greener pastures”, as the species itself liked to metaphorize. “It IS all a dream!” She exclaimed with budding hope. But, if it was all a dream, as she demanded it be, then the dream—as if with a mind of its own—decided, rather unceremoniously and uncharitably, “to be continued”. … .
