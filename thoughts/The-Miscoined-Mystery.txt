The Mis-Coined “Mystery”

Uncertain, yes. Unknown, sure. But these are negations, un-teeny gaps in our (now extremely stagnating) development. Mystery is supposed to represent an evenly-spaced riddle, a puzzle with clues, a neatly wrapped and bow-tied "problem to be solved”. Hitchcock accurately noted these as the hallmarks of “mystery”, separate and distinct from its close cousin “suspense”.

Yet I have met, for instance, innumerable people who know all sorts of really important things about their soon-to-be-lived lives from remarkably young ages. Whether or not they listen to these “inklings” is, it seems to me, where a story (and a storyteller) emerge. I’m surprised at how many ignore them, more often privileging the inklings of some random peer, or well-meaning parent, or faraway expert. And also thereby leaving the muscles of their own inner guidance to atrophy. This very atrophy then funnels back into the mystification process of all kinds of observable phenomena.  

If a mystery is just the result of “consciousness around the corner”, are all “revelations” that required its old-world, previously unshakeable foundation a lie, or just long-delayed collective gratification of that which was already hinted? When we say “how it will happen is a mystery” or that a case is “still a mystery” we are signifying a gap in our current understanding, not a gap in reality. Any gap can be filled, sometimes rapidly, sometimes dramatically. Experience seems to be the only thing that can fill it. It is the culmination of experience that could be said to be the true thrill of any so-called mystery. 

For a while now, I've spontaneously developed this “test” of sorts that lets me intuit quality in a story regardless of genre or form: if I cannot consistently predict the next scene, plot twist, dialogue, or action. That, to me, is the only real mystery worth noting - the “mystery” of actualized (and now very rare) creativity.
