The Comedian Above All

There is a pure moment in comedy; in a bit, a skit, a sketch, a moment quite rare, that force-halts you to a pause and expands time, whatever that is, like an invisible balloon. With newfound euphoria, it hangs your brain in the air with one gravity-less joyful moment you can feel from your throat to your toes. You can repeat the flash of its brilliance to someone else, rewatch it, play it back in your own head, remember the sensation but there it will never match the first time you heard it. Some you can see trailing from a mile away and plunk, alive on arrival, and some hit you on the head like a long-awaited ray of sunshine after a safe, preservationist ice age. 

There are the “you couldn’t write that” ones, ones you could only copy verbatim from the surprise and delight of actual life – they are that sublime, that pitch-perfect, that spontaneously, implausibly erupting. And yet that rational killjoy brain of yours knows it was cleverly conjured by the jester above. A ten-second zinger probably slaved over for two years. 

Like any creative expression, comedy is a translation of energy. How effective this translation is depends entirely on the level of mastery of the translator. Which does not necessarily mean that the humor itself is always intelligent – there is dumb, slapstick, misogynistic, boring, and did I mention dumb? Comedy that’s at somebody else’s expense. Shock. Physical. Heady. Linguistic. Situational. Burrowed out. Broad.  Improvisational. And new ones being born every day. But people respond to the comedian for the same reason they respond to the novelist. They are in essence waiting for the “that’s so true” moment. This moment exists in equal measure in both novels and stand-up – two forms that often only share as their common ground the currency of language but are otherwise in total opposition. One is silent, internal, solo, humble, while the other is shared, brash, loud and exhibitionist. Novels can exact a very refined sensation in the audience, breathing insights so precise and sharp you can almost hold them in your hand and remember them for a lifetime. In comedy, the effect is almost always less discriminatory, and more euphoric. Good novels invite complexity. Most comedy can very often seem as if it revels in the quashing of it. 

There is an inherent sexiness and verve to the seasoned comedian’s presence and likely no one more convinced of their inherent un-sexiness more so than any comedian. 

They often describe their art form as being as close to “justice” or “freedom” as a person can get. It’s immediate. Instant karma. Laughter or bust. Now that the novel has been completely ejected from mainstream relevance, it would seem as if the comedian, above all, is the last remaining conveyor (and purveyor) of our communal imagination.
